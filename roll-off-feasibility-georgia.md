# ROLL-OFF CONTAINER MANUFACTURING PLANT (GEORGIA / USA) — INVESTOR REPORT
## Phased Production Roadmap (Phase 1 → Phase 2 → Phase 3)

**Date:** August 2026 · **Currency:** All values are in US Dollars ($) unless otherwise stated.
**Scope:** Adaptation of the Turkey model (30 m³ heavy-duty roll-off container, three-phase phased production) to Georgia state market conditions.
**Document:** Investment decision support document — all figures are estimates based on August 2026 US/Georgia market data.

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This project establishes a manufacturing plant for **30 m³ (= ~39.2 yd³, matching the US standard 40 yd³ class) heavy-duty roll-off containers** (6,000 × 2,500 × 2,200 mm, open top, hook bar, slide rails). Production is built not in one go but in a **three-phase phased model**: each phase is financed from its own revenue, and every stage has a clear "go/no-go" decision gate. The model structure is identical to the Turkey report; only price, cost, tax and labor data reflect the Georgia/US market.

| Metric | Value (base case) |
|---|---|
| Total investment (3 phases) | **$3.37 million** |
| Phase 1 investment | **$1.30 million** |
| Year 1 unit sales | 268 units |
| Year 3 unit sales | 650 units |
| Unit production cost (Phase 1) | ~$12,354 |
| Average selling price | $14,500 → $15,800 (entry → normalization) |
| Annual EBITDA (Y1 / Y2 / Y3) | **$0.65M / $1.74M / $3.45M** |
| EBITDA margin | ~17% (entry) → ~34% (Y3) |
| Simple payback | **~4 years** (from production start) |
| Cumulative free cash flow (3 years) | **+$1.0M (~28% of investment)** |
| Cumulative cash return (5 years) | **~2.0× investment** |

**Why this product?** Roll-off containers serve the construction, excavation, municipal and industrial waste markets with steady demand — low-technology but high-value-added products. Production in Georgia competes with national manufacturers (Wastequip, Galbreath, Perkins) on **short lead time, customization, on-site warranty and regional logistics advantage**. Freight per container is high in the US; a regional producer beats national competitors' "factory + freight" offers.

**Market entry strategy (price):** Enter at **$14,500**, ~13% below regional competitors; growth comes from volume. Price normalizes to $15,000 in Y2 and $15,800 in Y3. With ~$12,083 unit cash cost, entry margin is ~17%; scale + automation push it to ~34% by Y3.

**Georgia vs Turkey:** The same business model in the US produces a longer payback (~4 years) due to higher labor, rent and insurance costs, no customs protection and strong national competition. In return: a deeper market, export via the Port of Savannah (Caribbean, Central America, Canada) and large-scale construction/excavation demand.

**Design approach:** Plates and sections are sourced **cut/bent** from an external supplier (Phase 1); welding and assembly are done in-house. Hooklift compatibility is modeled to US standards (hook type: Galbreath/Perkins compatible, skid length and hook opening).

## 4. PRODUCTION STRATEGY — THREE-PHASE ROADMAP

The model is not one big investment but **three independent phases**. Each phase finances the next from its own revenue, and each phase ends with a clear **decision gate**: if the condition is not met, no further investment is made and the operation is downsized and continued. Phases align to years — the investor sees at a glance what was spent in each phase and what revenue was generated up to that point.

### Phase comparison

| | Phase 1 | Phase 2 | Phase 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timing | Year 1 (months 1–12) | Year 2 (months 13–24) | Year 3 (months 25–36) |
| Capacity | 15–20 → 40 units/month | 45 units/month | 55–60 units/month |
| Staff | 15 (9 direct / 6 indirect) | 20 (12 / 8) | 19 (10 / 9) |
| Unit cash cost | ~$12,083 | ~$11,520 | ~$10,500 |
| Key change | Manual welding + outsourced cutting | CNC plasma brought in-house | Robotic welding deployed |

### Phase 1 — Year 1 (months 1–12): start with low investment

- **Investment:** $1.30M — workshop fit-out, gantry cranes (2 × 5 t), 2 welding stations (MIG/MAG), paint booth, portable cutting kit (~$20K), fixtures/jigs, initial stock.
- **Method / what changes:** Cutting and bending are done **at an external supplier** (no capital needed); in-house manual welding + assembly. Production starts at 15–20 units/month and ramps to ~36–40 by year-end. Building the customer portfolio realistically takes ~1 year; initially no staff is hired for 40 units/month (4 welders, 1 salesperson).
- **Objective:** Validate demand with real sales, learn the operation, build a customer/hook-type database.

**Financial picture up to this point (end of Year 1):**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Revenue | $3.89M (268 units × $14,500) |
| EBITDA | $0.65M (16.7%) |
| Cumulative investment (capex + working capital) | $1.30M |
| **Cumulative free cash flow** | **−$0.78M** |

**Decision gate 1 (month 12):** Sustainable sales **≥ 30 units/month** (ramp-end ~36) → proceed to Phase 2. Otherwise: reduce capacity, no further investment.

### Phase 2 — Year 2 (months 13–24): bring CNC plasma cutting in-house

- **Investment:** +$0.35M — CNC plasma + gas infrastructure (payback ~9 months).
- **What changes:** External cutting dependency **ends**; lead time shortens, unit cash cost drops from ~$12,083 to ~$11,520. Capacity rises to 45 units/month; welders 4 → 6, staff 20.

**Financial picture (end of Year 2):**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Cumulative revenue | $11.39M (3.89 + 7.50) |
| Cumulative EBITDA | $2.39M |
| Cumulative investment | $1.65M |
| **Cumulative free cash flow** | **+$0.22M** |

**Decision gate 2 (month 24):** Sustainable sales **≥ 45 units/month** → Phase 3.

### Phase 3 — Year 3 (months 25–36): welding robots and automation

- **Investment:** +$1.72M → **total $3.37M** ($1.30M + $0.35M + $1.72M)
  - 6 × 6-DOF welding robots: $0.68M; EOAT/section gripper + automatic torch changer (in-house design, material): $0.04M → subtotal $0.72M
  - Positioner/rollover fixtures (chassis turner, body rollover, door positioner, synchronized): $0.35M
  - Jigs/locating fixtures: $0.12M
  - Commissioning, training, contingency: $0.53M
- **What changes:** Robots take over welding labor → welders 6 → 2; one **robot operator** and a welding engineer are added (staff 20 → 19). Unit cash cost drops to ~$10,500, capacity rises to 55–60 units/month; consistent weld quality and resilience to the US welder shortage.

**Financial picture (end of Year 3):**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Cumulative revenue | $21.66M |
| Cumulative EBITDA | $5.83M |
| Cumulative investment | $3.37M |
| **Cumulative free cash flow** | **+$1.0M (~28% of investment)** |

> **Why phased?** Robots create a depreciation burden at low volume (one-off full automation can cost more than manual at low sales). Each phase is financed from its own cash flow; the investor has no one-time sunk-risk exposure.

## 6. PRICING AND REVENUE

### Competitive entry price (penetration)
- **Entry price:** **$14,500/unit** — ~13% below regional custom competitors (~$16,700). In the heavy-duty (6 mm floor / 4 mm wall) custom segment, the US price band is $10,000–16,000; the entry price sits at the top of this band, below competitors.
- **Growth engine is volume, not price:** a competitive price starts market share and references; scale lowers unit cost. The regional producer advantage is defended with the "factory + freight" difference.
- **Normalization path:** Y2 $15,000 → Y3 $15,800 (gradual once brand and references settle).
- Options: rear door, custom size, corporate logo, custom paint → +$350–900/unit.

**Sales channels:** Solid waste companies (WM, Republic Services, Waste Pro, regional players), municipalities and public tenders (Georgia 4% local production preference), construction/excavation contractors, co-selling with hooklift truck dealers, container rental companies.

## 8. SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS

| Scenario | EBITDA margin | Payback estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Base | ~17% (entry) → ~34% (Y3) | ~4 years |
| Price −15% | ~2% | 6+ years (near negative) |
| Volume −25% | ~12% | ~5.5 years |
| Price −15% and volume −25% | Negative EBITDA | Unsustainable |

The model is most sensitive to **price**. Without customs protection in the US, price competition directly determines margin; a +20% steel price raises unit cost from ~$12,354 to ~$13,400 and must be protected by price adjustment (steel indexation clauses in contracts). Therefore, in the Georgia model, **staying in the heavy-duty/custom niche segment** and not cutting price for volume is critical.

## 12. INVESTOR VALUE PROPOSITION

1. **Low-risk start:** Production begins with $1.30M; $3.37M is not committed until demand is proven.
2. **Self-financing scale:** Later phases are financed from the current phase's cash flow; external borrowing needs are low (positive cumulative cash at end of Phase 2/Y2).
3. **Growing regional market:** Atlanta metro construction/excavation demand, the Port of Savannah (US 4th largest container port) and large-scale industrial investments (automotive, battery, solar) fuel waste and container demand in Georgia.
4. **Export base:** Container exports to the Caribbean, Central America and Canada via Savannah and Brunswick; shorter transit than national producers.
5. **Regional logistics advantage:** An empty container is heavy; the "factory + freight" difference favors a regional producer.
6. **Competitive entry, scaling margin:** ~17% entry margin (Y1) → ~34% normalized margin (Y3); ~4-year simple payback.
7. **Clear exit options:** Stop/downsize option at each phase; limited sunk risk.
8. **Tax and financing tools:** Federal 21% + Georgia 4.99% (effective ~25%); accelerated depreciation on manufacturing equipment (Section 179 / bonus depreciation) and SBA 7(a) equipment financing.
